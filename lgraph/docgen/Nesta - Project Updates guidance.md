## 2\. Project updates

Project updates are used to inform targeted Nesta audiences about our programmes and activities related to them. Updates include announcements, such as the launch of a new fund and reflective writing, for example sharing periodic insights or lessons learnt.

## Best practice:

### 1\. Grab the reader’s attention

Always state the purpose of the update in the first paragraph. Make it clear why the reader should continue reading by answering the ‘so what?’ question at the start.

Remember readers online decide in the first few seconds if they will continue to read on. Do not keep them guessing. Think about why this information is useful beyond an internal Nesta audience.

### 2\. Avoid scroll fatigue

Updates may vary in length but should always be written for the web. This means breaking up lengthy text with subsection heading, quotes and/or images.

Remember most users will be put off by scrolling through lengthy paragraphs.

### 3\. Accessibility

All content on the Nesta website should be written in accessible language.

Remember to avoid jargon. Always explain terms and write out abbreviations in full in the first instance.

### 4\. SEO friendly titles

Search engine optimization allows your content to be easily found on the web. Make sure your titles are short, snappy and descriptive.

Remember Google cannot decipher puns, so avoid cryptic wording and opt for titles that would work well as a tweet.

*For example*

* Think of a statement or question that outlines your argument e.g. ‘Why innovative practice needs a shake up’
* Use numbers ‘5 ways to…. 10 reasons why…’

Keep titles to less than 10 words and subtitles to no more than 15 words.
